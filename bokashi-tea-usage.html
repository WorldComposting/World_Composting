Bokashi Tea: How to Use Fermentation Liquid as Plant Fertilizer

Bokashi fermentation produces liquid tea that is a powerful plant fertilizer. Learn how to dilute, apply, and store it safely.

Contents

  • What Is Bokashi Tea?
  • Dilution Ratios for Different Plants
  • Application Methods
  • Storage and Shelf Life

What Is Bokashi Tea?

Every time you drain liquid from your bokashi bucket, you are collecting bokashi tea — a nutrient-rich fermentation liquid packed with beneficial bacteria, enzymes, and dissolved nutrients. It is essentially a liquid fertilizer that can boost plant growth.

Dilution Ratios for Different Plants

  • General garden plants: Dilute 1 part tea to 50 parts water (1:50)
  • Sensitive seedlings: Dilute 1 part tea to 100 parts water (1:100)
  • Fertigation (drip irrigation): Dilute 1 part tea to 200 parts water (1:200)

The key is always diluting. Undiluted bokashi tea can burn plant roots due to its acidity and high nutrient concentration.

Application Methods

  1. Spray on leaves: Dilute at 1:100 and mist onto foliage as a foliar feed.
  2. Soil drench: Water around the base of plants with 1:50 dilution.
  3. Fertigation: Add to drip irrigation systems at 1:200 for continuous feeding.

Storage and Shelf Life

Bokashi tea is best used fresh. Store it in a sealed container away from sunlight and use within one week. If it develops mold or smells putrid, discard it.
