Lasagna Compost for Soil Building: How to Create Rich Garden Bed

Learn how lasagna composting builds fertile garden beds from the ground up — layer by layer, no digging required.

Contents

  • The No-Dig Advantage
  • Step-by-Step Building Guide
  • Decomposition Timeline
  • When to Plant

The No-Dig Advantage

Lasagna composting is a no-dig method. You build your garden bed directly on top of existing soil or grass, layer by layer. The decomposition happens in place, creating rich, fertile soil without any tilling or turning.

Step-by-Step Building Guide

  1. Clear the area — remove grass and weeds (or just mow them short)
  2. Add a cardboard layer (no tape or staples) to suppress weeds
  3. Layer soil/compost (4 inches), then greens, browns, repeat
  4. Build up to 3-4 feet tall over several weeks
  5. Top with soil and let it settle for 2-6 months before planting

Decomposition Timeline

  • Month 1: Active decomposition, heat builds in the center
  • Months 2-3: Pile settles and shrinks by about half its original height
  • Months 4-6: Fully decomposed, dark and crumbly — ready for planting

When to Plant

You can plant directly into a lasagna bed after it has settled (usually 2-3 months). For heavy feeders like tomatoes, wait until the pile is fully decomposed (4-6 months) so roots do not burn from residual heat.
