Best Bedding Materials for Worm Bins

Compare shredded paper, coconut coir, aged leaves and more to find the ideal bedding for your worm bin.

In This Guide

  1. Why Bedding Matters
  2. Top Bedding Materials Compared
  3. How to Prepare Your Bedding
  4. Maintaining Bedding Over Time
  5. Quick Reference: Bedding Material Guide

Bedding is the foundation of any successful worm bin. It provides shelter, moisture retention, and a carbon source for your red wigglers to thrive. Choosing the right bedding material can mean the difference between a thriving colony and a struggling one.

Why Bedding Matters

Your worms live in the bedding layer, not just the food scraps you add. Good bedding serves multiple purposes: it creates air pockets for oxygen flow, holds moisture without becoming waterlogged, buffers pH changes from acidic foods, and gives worms surfaces to burrow through. Without proper bedding, your bin can become compacted, smelly, or too wet.

Top Bedding Materials Compared

Shredded Newspaper and Cardboard

This is the most common and accessible bedding option. Both materials are high in carbon, inexpensive, and easy to source. Shred newspaper into strips about one inch wide and soak it in water before adding to your bin. Cardboard works similarly but takes longer to break down.

Pros: Cheap, readily available, excellent moisture retention, biodegradable

Cons: Glossy or colored ink may contain toxins (stick to black and white), breaks down slowly over time, requires soaking before use

Coconut Coir

Coconut coir is a natural fiber extracted from coconut husks. It has excellent water retention properties and a light, fluffy texture that worms love. Many vermicomposting experts consider it one of the best bedding materials available.

Pros: Superior moisture control, lightweight, sustainable resource, resists compaction

Cons: More expensive than paper products, requires rehydration from compressed blocks, not as carbon-rich as newspaper

Aged Leaves and Leaf Litter

Fallen leaves collected in autumn make excellent natural bedding. Chop or shred them into small pieces and soak before adding to the bin. Aged leaves that have already begun decomposing are ideal since they are less likely to contain tannins that can acidify your bin.

Pros: Free, natural, adds biodiversity to your worm habitat, excellent structure

Cons: Seasonal availability, may contain pesticides if from treated lawns, requires preparation time

Peanut Butter and Egg Cartons

Unbleached paper egg cartons and cardboard peanut butter trays are convenient bedding options that worms can eat as they break down. These materials provide good structure and air pockets.

Pros: Readily available, worms consume them directly, good aeration

Cons: Limited supply for large bins, may not hold moisture as well as other options

How to Prepare Your Bedding

Regardless of which material you choose, proper preparation is essential. Here is the standard process:

  1. Shred or chop your chosen bedding into small pieces (one inch or smaller)
  2. Soak the material in water for several hours until fully saturated
  3. Squeeze out excess water until the bedding feels like a wrung-out sponge
  4. Fluff up the material to create air pockets before adding to your bin

Maintaining Bedding Over Time

Your bedding will break down as worms process it and new layers are added. You should top off your bedding every few weeks by adding fresh shredded material on top of the existing layer. This maintains depth, provides fresh carbon sources, and prevents the bin from becoming too compacted.
