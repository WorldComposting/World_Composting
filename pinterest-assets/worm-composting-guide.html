Worm Composting Guide

Everything you need to know about feeding your red wigglers

🟢 Greens vs 🍂 Browns — The Golden Ratio

A healthy worm bin needs the right balance of nitrogen-rich "greens" and carbon-rich "browns." Aim for a ratio of roughly 1:3 (greens to browns).

🥬

Greens (Nitrogen)

Fruit scraps, veggie peels, coffee grounds, tea bags, fresh grass clippings

🍂

Browns (Carbon)

Dry leaves, shredded paper, cardboard, sawdust, straw, egg cartons

Food TypeGreens/BrownsNotes
Citrus peelsGreenLimited — too acidic in large amounts
Coffee groundsGreenGreat! Also adds nitrogen and worms love them
Shredded paperBrownUse black ink only, no glossy pages
Eggshells (crushed)NeutralAdds calcium and grit for worm digestion
Meat/DairyAvoidAttracts pests, causes odors

Pro Tip: When in doubt, add more browns! A smelly bin is usually a sign of too many greens.

🍽️ What Your Worms Love

  • Fruit & veggie scraps: Apple cores, banana peels, carrot tops, lettuce ends
  • Coffee grounds & tea bags: Worms eat these fast — great for quick composting
  • Grain leftovers: Cooked rice, pasta, bread (in small amounts)
  • Eggshells: Crush them fine — adds grit and calcium

🚫 What to Avoid

  • Meat, fish, dairy (attracts pests)
  • Oily or greasy foods
  • Citrus in large quantities (too acidic)
  • Onions & garlic (can harm worms in excess)
  • Pet waste (pathogens)

📐 How to Layer Your Bin

  1. Bottom layer: Shredded newspaper or cardboard (moistened)
  2. Add worms on top of the bedding
  3. Bury food scraps under a thin layer of fresh bedding each time you feed
  4. Top with more bedding to keep moisture in and flies out

Rule of thumb: For every cup of food scraps, add 2–3 cups of brown bedding on top.

📅 Feeding Schedule

TimeframeAmount
New bin (first 2 weeks)No food — let worms settle in bedding
Weeks 3–4Small scraps, once per week
Established bin1/2 to 1 cup of scraps per day (depends on worm count)

Signs you're overfeeding: Unpleasant odor, fruit flies, worms trying to escape. Reduce food and add more browns.
