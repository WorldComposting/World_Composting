Why Is My Compost Pile Not Heating Up?

Troubleshoot a cold compost pile by checking pile size, moisture, carbon-to-nitrogen balance, aeration, and temperature.

By Newell Davis, World Composting. More than 30 years of composting experience, with hands-on testing across worm bins, hot composting, and other systems. About the author.

A hot compost pile needs enough material, moisture, nitrogen, and oxygen for microbial activity to build heat. If a pile stays cool, check these conditions in order before adding more material.

Is the pile large enough?

Small piles lose heat quickly. A pile needs enough mass to retain the heat generated by microbial activity. If your pile is small, build it up with a balanced mix of fresh greens and dry browns rather than expecting a small container to behave like a large hot pile.

Check moisture

The center should feel like a wrung-out sponge. If it is dry, add water gradually while turning so moisture spreads evenly. If it is soggy, add dry browns and improve drainage before trying to heat it again.

Check the mix

A pile made mostly of dry leaves, straw, or woody material may lack available nitrogen. A pile made mostly of wet greens may become compacted and oxygen-starved. Mix nitrogen-rich greens with carbon-rich browns and avoid dense layers.

Restore oxygen

Turn or loosen a pile that is compacted or smells sour. Turning can temporarily lower the temperature, but it restores oxygen and redistributes material. Check the core again after the pile has had time to reheat.

Use a thermometer and be patient

Measure the center of the pile rather than judging by the surface. Temperature changes with pile size, weather, ingredients, and management. If the pile is active but not hot, it can still decompose; not every compost system needs to reach thermophilic temperatures.
