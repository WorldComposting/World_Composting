Tumbler Compost Not Heating Up: Troubleshooting Guide

Diagnose the root cause — wrong greens-to-browns ratio, insufficient volume, or lack of turning.

Contents

  • Insufficient Volume
  • Wrong Greens-to-Browns Ratio
  • Not Turning Enough
  • Lack of Insulation
  • Quick Fix Checklist

Insufficient Volume

Tumblers need at least 3-4 cubic feet of material to generate enough heat from microbial activity. If your tumbler is only half full, it will not reach thermophilic temperatures (above 130°F).

Wrong Greens-to-Browns Ratio

  • If you have too many browns (dry leaves, cardboard), decomposition slows and heat drops.
  • Add more greens — fruit scraps, coffee grounds, fresh grass clippings.

Not Turning Enough

Tumblers rely on rotation to introduce oxygen. If you only turn once a week or less, the center goes anaerobic and heat production stops.

  • Turn at least every 2-3 days during active decomposition.
  • Check temperature with a compost thermometer — aim for 130-160°F in the center.

Lack of Insulation

In cold weather, tumblers lose heat rapidly. Wrap your tumbler with an old blanket or place it in a shaded area to retain warmth.

Quick Fix Checklist

  1. Add more material until the tumbler is at least half full
  2. Increase greens ratio — add fruit scraps or coffee grounds
  3. Turn every 2-3 days instead of weekly
  4. Check temperature with a thermometer
  5. Wrap in insulation during cold weather
