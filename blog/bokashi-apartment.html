Bokashi Composting for Apartments: Complete Guide to Fermentation

How to compost all food waste indoors using the bokashi fermentation method, perfect for apartments and small spaces.

Introduction

Unlike traditional composting, bokashi lets you compost ALL food waste indoors — including meat, dairy, and oily foods — with zero odor when sealed properly. This makes it ideal for apartment dwellers who want to reduce household waste but don't have outdoor space.

How Bokashi Works

Bokashi (Japanese for "fermented organic matter") works by creating an anaerobic (oxygen-free) environment where beneficial microbes ferment food waste rather than decomposing it. Think of it like making sauerkraut with your kitchen scraps.

The Process

  1. Layer food waste: Add scraps to a sealed bucket.
  2. Cover with bokashi bran: Each layer is sprinkled with inoculated bran containing EM bacteria.
  3. Seal tight: The seal keeps oxygen out, creating anaerobic conditions.
  4. Ferment 2-4 weeks: Over time, the microbes ferment everything inside — turning it into pre-compost.
  5. Bury in soil: The output is buried where aerobic decomposition completes the process.

Setting Up Your Bokashi System

What You Need

  • Bokashi bucket: Food-grade 5-gallon bucket with a tight-sealing lid and a spigot near the bottom to drain liquid (bokashi tea).
  • Bokashi bran: Contains EM-1 (Effective Microorganisms) — the bacteria that do the fermentation. Available online or you can make your own using molasses, flour, and water.
  • Food scraps: Everything goes in — fruit, vegetables, meat, dairy, bread, cooked food, small bones.

Optional but Helpful

  • Second bucket (to rotate while one ferments)
  • Compostable paper bags for portioning scraps
  • Airtight container for storing bokashi bran

Setting Up Your First Bokashi Bucket

  1. Place the bucket: Under your kitchen sink or in a convenient location.
  2. Line the bottom: Add a layer of shredded paper (optional, helps absorb liquid).
  3. Add food scraps: Start adding scraps in small batches throughout the week.
  4. Sprinkle bran: After each addition, sprinkle 1-2 tablespoons of bokashi bran over the top.
  5. Press and seal: Press down firmly to remove air pockets and close the lid tightly.
  6. Drain regularly: Open the spigot every few days to drain liquid (bokashi tea — dilute 1:100 for plant fertilizer).

How Long Until It's Ready?

  • Minimum: 2 weeks in warm weather, 4 weeks in cold weather.
  • The waste will look unrecognizable and smell pickled (not rotten) when ready.
  • If you're unsure, err on the side of longer fermentation — it won't hurt anything.

Using Your Bokashi Output

Method 1: Bury in Garden Beds (Recommended)

  1. Dig a trench: 6-8 inches deep in your garden bed or planting area.
  2. Spread output: Distribute the fermented bokashi evenly.
  3. Cover with soil: At least 4+ inches to prevent flies and odors.
  4. Wait 2-4 weeks: For final decomposition before planting.

Method 2: Add to Outdoor Compost Bin

  • Mix bokashi output directly into your outdoor compost pile or bin.
  • The aerobic microbes in the existing pile will finish decomposition within 2-4 weeks.
  • Ideal for apartment dwellers who have access to a community garden or shared composting area.

Video: Bokashi Composting in a Worm Bin — 25th week update

Method 3: Indoor Potted Plant Integration

  • For apartment dwellers without outdoor space, mix bokashi output into large indoor potted plants.
  • Bury at least 4 inches deep to prevent any odor or pest issues.
  • Wait 2-4 weeks before planting new seeds in the same pot.

Bokashi Tea: The Liquid Gold

  • Dilute 1:100 with water for plant fertilization.
  • Undiluted: Pour on soil to inoculate with beneficial microbes and reduce odors in drains.
  • Smell: Strong, sour, pickled odor — this means it's working!

Troubleshooting Bokashi Issues

Too Much Liquid

  • Cause: Adding too much wet food at once or not draining regularly.
  • Fix: Drain the spigot more frequently (every 1-2 days). Reduce watery scraps like watermelon and cucumber.

Bad Smell (Not Pickled)

  • Cause: Oxygen getting into the bucket — seal is broken or lid isn't tight enough.
  • Fix: Check the gasket around the lid, ensure it seals completely. Add more bran to restart fermentation.

Flies Appearing

  • Cause: Food exposed at the top of the bucket.
  • Fix: Cover all food scraps with a layer of shredded paper or used bokashi bran before closing the lid.

Output Is Too Wet

  • Cause: Not enough carbon material in the bucket.
  • Fix: Add dry materials like shredded paper, sawdust, or dry leaves to balance moisture levels.

Advanced Bokashi Techniques

Making Your Own Bokashi Bran

You can make bokashi bran at home for a fraction of the cost of buying it:

  1. Mix solution: 1 cup EM-1 concentrate + 1 cup molasses + 1 gallon water.
  2. Spray onto bran: Spray the solution onto 5 pounds of wheat bran or sawdust.
  3. Mix thoroughly: Then seal in a bucket for 7-14 days to inoculate.
  4. Store: Keep finished bran in an airtight container — lasts up to 6 months.

Two-Bucket Rotation System

For continuous use without waiting:

  1. Fill Bucket A: Add scraps throughout the week.
  2. Seal and ferment: When full, seal for 2-4 weeks.
  3. Start Bucket B: Meanwhile, begin filling a second bucket.
  4. Empty and reuse: Once Bucket A is ready, empty it into your garden or compost pile and reuse it.

Bokashi + Vermicomposting: The Perfect Pair

Bokashi output and worm bins complement each other beautifully:

  • Bokashi pre-digests food that would be too much for worms (meat, dairy, large quantities).
  • After burying bokashi output for 2-4 weeks, the resulting compost is ideal worm bedding.
  • Many experienced composters use both systems together for maximum waste reduction.
