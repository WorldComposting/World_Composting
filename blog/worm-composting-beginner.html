How to Start a Worm Bin Indoors: Complete Beginner's Guide

Everything you need to know about setting up your first indoor worm composting system, from choosing the right bin to feeding and maintaining healthy worms.

Introduction

You don't need a yard or a garden to turn food scraps into black gold. Vermicomposting (worm composting) is one of the most popular indoor composting methods because it works well under the kitchen sink, in a basement, or closet — no smell and no pests when done right.

This guide covers everything: choosing your bin, getting worms, setup, feeding, troubleshooting, and harvesting nutrient-rich castings that are 5x more valuable than regular compost.

Why Worm Composting Works Indoors

Vermicomposting uses red wigglers (Eisenia fetida) that thrive in contained environments. Unlike hot composting, worm bins operate at ambient temperatures — perfect for basements, under sinks, or closets.

The end product: nutrient-rich castings packed with beneficial microbes, plant-available nutrients, and humic acids that improve soil structure and water retention.

What to Look For in Any Bin

  • Drainage: Must have a spigot or easy drainage for leachate.
  • Aeration: Holes drilled in lid and sides for airflow.
  • Size: Start with 10 gallons — enough capacity without being overwhelming.
  • Material: Opaque fabric or plastic (worms avoid light).

Getting Your Worms

SpeciesTemperature RangeBest Use
Red Wiggler (Eisenia fetida)55-77°F (13-25°C)Indoor bins, beginners
African Nightcrawler60-85°F (15-29°C)Warmer climates, outdoor
European Nightcrawler45-75°F (7-24°C)Cooler basements, garages

Recommendation for beginners: Red wigglers are the gold standard. They're hardy, reproduce quickly, and handle a wide range of conditions.

How Many Worms to Start With

1 pound of worms (roughly 1,000 red wigglers) is the ideal starting amount. This gives you enough population to process about half a pound of food scraps per week.

Setting Up Your Bin

  1. Choose your location: Under the sink, in a basement, closet, or garage. Keep it out of direct sunlight and away from extreme temperatures.
  2. Prepare bedding: Shred 4-5 sheets of newspaper into confetti-sized pieces. Soak in water until wrung-out damp (like a sponge). Add to your bin — this should fill about half the volume.
  3. Add worms: Place worms on top of the bedding and let them burrow down naturally. Don't mix them in.
  4. Initial feeding: Cover the bedding with a thin layer of kitchen scraps (no more than 1/4 cup for a new bin).
  5. Wait one week: Let the system establish before adding more food.

Feeding Your Worms

What to Feed (The Greens)

  • Fruit and vegetable scraps (banana peels, apple cores, carrot tops)
  • Coffee grounds and tea bags (remove staples)
  • Crushed eggshells (adds calcium, balances pH)
  • Leftover grains in small amounts

What NOT to Feed (Avoid These)

  • Meat, fish, dairy — causes odors and pests
  • Oily or greasy foods — attracts flies
  • Citrus in large quantities — too acidic
  • Spicy foods, onions, garlic — can harm worms
  • Glossy or colored paper — contains chemicals

Feeding Schedule

  • Week 1: No food (system establishment)
  • Weeks 2-4: Start with 1/4 cup every 3 days
  • Month 2+: Gradually increase to 1/2 lb per week
  • Rule of thumb: Feed less than what they can eat in a week

Troubleshooting Common Problems

Fruit Flies

  • Cause: Food exposed on the surface.
  • Fix: Bury food 2 inches deep under bedding. Add a layer of damp newspaper or cardboard.

Video: Get Rid of Fruit Flies in Worm Bins

Worms Trying to Escape

  • Cause: Usually too wet, too acidic, or no food.
  • Fix: Add dry bedding (shredded paper), sprinkle crushed eggshells for pH balance, and ensure adequate food supply.

Video: Worms Escaping!

Bin Smells Bad

  • Cause: Overfeeding or too much nitrogen (greens).
  • Fix: Stop feeding for a few days, add dry browns (shredded paper, cardboard), and mix the bin. Add biochar to absorb excess moisture.

Worms Are Lethargic or Dying

  • Cause: Temperature extremes, chemical exposure, or overfeeding.
  • Fix: Check temperature (ideal: 55-77°F), remove any spoiled food, add fresh bedding.

Harvesting Your Castings

When to Harvest

  • After 3-6 months, when the bedding has turned dark and crumbly.
  • You'll notice fewer worms in the food layer — they've moved down.

The Light Method (Easiest for Beginners)

  1. Push all contents to one side of the bin.
  2. Add fresh bedding and food to the empty side.
  3. After 1-2 weeks, worms will have migrated to the new side.
  4. Remove the castings from the original side — it should be mostly worm-free.
