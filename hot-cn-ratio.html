C:N Ratio Guide for Hot Composting

Balance greens and browns with the carbon to nitrogen ratio: reference tables, mixing rules, and troubleshooting.

The carbon to nitrogen ratio, or C:N ratio, is the single most important factor in successful hot composting. It determines how fast your pile heats up, whether it produces odors, and how quickly it finishes decomposing. Understanding this ratio gives you control over the entire composting process.

What Is a C:N Ratio?

The C:N ratio expresses the proportion of carbon atoms to nitrogen atoms in organic material. Carbon provides energy for bacteria, while nitrogen provides protein for bacterial growth and reproduction. The balance between these two elements determines how efficiently microbes break down your compost materials.

The Ideal Ratio for Hot Composting

A C:N ratio of 25 to 30 parts carbon to one part nitrogen is the sweet spot for hot composting. At this ratio, bacteria multiply rapidly enough to generate heat while having sufficient energy to sustain decomposition over several weeks.

How to Calculate Your Pile Ratio

You do not need a laboratory to balance your compost pile. Use this practical method:

  1. Measure your greens and browns by volume (cubic feet or buckets)
  2. Use the rule of thumb: mix two parts brown material with one part green
  3. Adjust based on your materials: if using sawdust (very high carbon), use three parts brown to one part green
  4. If using mostly grass clippings (low carbon), reduce browns to one and a half parts per one part green

When the Ratio Is Off Balance

SymptomRatio ProblemFix
Pile is not heating upToo much carbon (ratio too high)Add more greens: grass clippings, manure, or kitchen scraps
Pile smells like ammoniaToo much nitrogen (ratio too low)Add more browns: leaves, straw, or shredded cardboard
Pile is slimy and wetExcess nitrogen with poor aerationAdd dry browns and turn the pile thoroughly
Pile is dry and dustyHigh carbon with insufficient moistureAdd water and mix in some greens to boost nitrogen

Seasonal Adjustments

Your C:N ratio needs change with the seasons. In spring and summer, higher temperatures accelerate decomposition so a slightly lower carbon ratio (closer to 20:1) works well. In fall and winter, slower bacterial activity means you need more carbon material (closer to 35:1) to sustain heat generation.
