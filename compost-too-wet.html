How to Fix Compost That Is Too Wet

Learn how to diagnose and fix overly wet compost, including worm bins, tumblers, and outdoor compost piles.

By Newell Davis, World Composting. More than 30 years of composting experience, with hands-on testing across worm bins, hot composting, and other systems. About the author.

Compost needs moisture, but saturated material loses air spaces and can become compacted and anaerobic. The fix is to absorb excess water, restore structure, and identify why the system became wet.

Check the moisture level

A handful of active compost should feel like a wrung-out sponge: moist, but not dripping. If water runs out when squeezed, or the material feels slimy and dense, it is too wet. Worm-bin bedding should be moist throughout without standing liquid.

Add dry, absorbent browns

Mix in dry shredded cardboard, torn egg cartons, dry leaves, or untreated paper. Add a little at a time, mix gently, and reassess. In a worm bin, fluff the bedding and add dry bedding around wet food pockets. Avoid adding large quantities of sawdust or fine material that can compact.

Improve drainage and airflow

For outdoor piles, protect the pile from heavy rain while allowing airflow and make sure water can drain from the base. For tumblers, avoid adding wet material in large clumps and turn regularly. For worm bins, check drainage holes and empty collection trays.

Adjust what goes in

Drain very wet food scraps, add dry browns alongside juicy fruit and vegetable waste, and reduce feeding while the system catches up. Do not add more water until the moisture level is back in range.

Watch for warning signs

Persistent rotten odors, slimy material, or pests indicate the system needs more than a quick sprinkle of browns. Remove any obviously spoiled pockets and restore a loose, aerated structure.
