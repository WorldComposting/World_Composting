How to Get Rid of Fruit Flies in Compost

Find out why fruit flies appear around compost and worm bins, how to stop breeding at the source, and how to prevent them returning.

By Newell Davis, World Composting. More than 30 years of composting experience, with hands-on testing across worm bins, hot composting, and other systems. About the author.

Fruit flies are attracted to exposed, fermenting fruit and vegetable scraps. The lasting solution is to remove access to food and breeding sites, not simply to kill the adult flies.

Bury and cover fresh scraps

In a worm bin, place food in a small pocket and cover it completely with several inches of bedding. Rotate feeding locations and avoid leaving fruit on the surface. In outdoor piles, cover fresh kitchen scraps with a layer of dry leaves, finished compost, or other carbon-rich material.

Reduce excess food and moisture

If old scraps are still visible, pause feeding until the colony catches up. Remove any large, soured food pockets. Add dry shredded cardboard or leaves if the material is overly wet. A wet, food-heavy surface is especially attractive to flies.

Use a physical barrier

A layer of damp newspaper, cardboard, or a breathable worm-bin cover can make it harder for flies to reach scraps. Keep ventilation openings clear; do not seal a system that needs airflow.

Catch adults while fixing the source

A small vinegar trap can reduce adult flies nearby, but it will not solve the infestation if scraps remain exposed. Keep traps away from pets and children, and continue covering food until the adult population declines.

Prevent a repeat

Freeze or pre-compost fruit scraps before adding them if fruit flies are a recurring problem. Thaw scraps before feeding, add them in small portions, and keep the top bedding layer mostly free of food.
