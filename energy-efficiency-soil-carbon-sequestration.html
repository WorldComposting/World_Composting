Compost for Soil Health and Carbon Sequestration

How composting builds soil health, sequesters carbon, and improves long-term garden productivity.

Soil Health Fundamentals

Healthy soil is alive. A single teaspoon of healthy garden soil contains more microorganisms than there are people on Earth. Compost introduces and feeds these organisms, building a resilient ecosystem below the surface.

Carbon Sequestration Potential

  • Compost applied to soil locks carbon in a stable form for decades — far longer than leaving it as raw organic waste
  • A well-managed compost operation can sequester approximately 1-3 tons of CO2 equivalent per acre per year

Regenerative Composting Practices

  1. Avoid turning compost too frequently — let it mature undisturbed for maximum carbon stabilization
  2. Mix in biochar to increase the soil's long-term carbon storage capacity

Long-Term Garden Productivity

Gardens amended with compost year after year show measurable improvements: increased water retention, better nutrient availability, reduced pest pressure, and higher crop yields. The investment in compost pays dividends for decades.
